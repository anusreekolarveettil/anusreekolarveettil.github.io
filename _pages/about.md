I am a PhD candidate in the Department of Civil Engineering at the Indian Institute of Science (IISc), Bangalore, supervised by Prof. G. Madhavi Latha. I study how particle shape governs the packing, flow and mechanical behaviour of granular soils. My work combines 3D printing, X-ray micro-computed tomography (micro-CT), laboratory experiments and discrete element method (DEM) simulations.

## Research

Natural sand grains vary in shape at several scales at once (overall form, angularity and surface roughness), which makes it hard to isolate the effect of any one of them in the laboratory. We use 3D printing as a way to make granular materials with controlled shapes, so that each level of morphology can be studied on its own.

- **Shape characterisation and printing fidelity.** A workflow involving micro-CT scanning, image processing, spherical harmonic reconstruction, shape characterisaion and 3D printing, with an evaluation of how faithfully SLA, DLP and PolyJet printers reproduce natural sand morphology.
- **Packing and permeability.** Experiments on 3D-printed sand surrogates showing how particle form and angularity control packing density and permeability.
- **Transparent granular media.** Transparent sand made from 3D-printed particles, used to measure pore-scale flow with particle image velocimetry (PIV) in a custom-built setup.
- **Discrete Element Modelling.** PFC3D simulations of dense and loose packings across particle shapes, compared with packing experiments on the corresponding printed grains.
